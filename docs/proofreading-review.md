# Textbook Proofreading Review

The [November 2 adversarial audit](#post-transcription-audit) below inventories every committed revision since the first complete transcription, records the new review's fixes and limits, and assesses vocabulary and Anki coverage. Earlier sections retain the scope and counts of their original review snapshots.

The Markdown transcription is substantially improved, but the original textbook contains incomplete explanations and inconsistent examples. Clear spelling, transcription, and linguistic errors have been corrected. Remaining substantive defects are marked visibly in the affected sections and listed below; this review does not certify every Malayalam sentence as error free.

Reviewed on October 3, 2026 against *Malayalam: A University Course and Reference Grammar*, fourth edition, by Rodney F. Moag, using the local 557-page PDF. Three parallel reviewers covered Lessons 1–9, 10–18, and 19–25 plus the minilessons; the coordinating review covered front matter, script tables, appendices, glossary, index, and structural consistency.

## Corrections Made

English spelling, punctuation, quotation marks, spacing, and several incorrect section references were corrected throughout. Page comments and historical wording were preserved, except for clear errors. Substantive corrections and new learner guidance are identified as editorial material.

Malayalam fixes include malformed vowel markers, duplicated or missing letters, and the legacy doubled റ spelling, now encoded as the conjunct റ്റ throughout. For example, the glossary now agrees with lesson text on മറ്റുള്ളവർ, പറ്റില്ല, and നൂറ്റാണ്ട്. The alphabet page explains this Unicode conversion. This does not modernize every historical or dialect spelling.

## Teaching Sequence

The audit checked whether new words and forms were supported by earlier instruction, the current lesson’s vocabulary, translated examples, or a usable exercise model. Exact surface forms were also compared with earlier lessons and glossary entries to find candidates for manual review. That comparison cannot reliably analyze all inflections, compounds, meanings, or intended contextual inference.

The book deliberately introduces some expressions as vocabulary chunks before explaining their grammar. Its [usage instructions](usage.md) tell readers to consult the relevant grammar note before an exercise, even though grammar notes appear later in the file. These are legitimate teaching choices. Glossary lesson numbers are not a dependable inventory of first occurrence: the source explicitly excludes some grammar-example and reference-list vocabulary. The glossary’s introductory claim has been clarified accordingly.

## Historical and Factual Context

Added a period-context note to the [preface](preface.md). Statements about population, literacy, education, newspaper circulation, and social relationships should be read as the author’s 2002 account. They have not all been checked or updated to present-day conditions.

The preface incorrectly locates Elamite in the Arabian peninsula. It was spoken in southwestern Iran; its proposed relationship to Dravidian remains a hypothesis. An editorial correction links to [Gernot L. Windfuhr’s linguistic sketch](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iran-vii3-elamite/). Its description of Hindi as a national language is also corrected with reference to the [Government of India’s official language provisions and policy](https://rajbhasha.gov.in/en/bharat-samhita-code-of-india-).

[Lesson 11](lesson11.md) now identifies the source’s erroneous placement of Kanyakumari in Kerala, citing the [official Tamil Nadu district website](https://kanniyakumari.nic.in/about-district/). [Lesson 10](lesson10.md) flags outdated northern-district exercise sentences that conflict with its own revised geography text. [Lesson 16](lesson16.md) flags historical state-count and newspaper-publication claims. These practice texts remain identifiable as source material.

## Verification and Limits

Structural checks cover all textbook Markdown files: all 557 PDF page markers occur exactly once; local Markdown links and images exist; linked heading fragments resolve; table rows match their header widths; no Unicode replacement characters remain. Markdown was parsed with the repository’s cached Marked package, and source-page comments remain hidden in the generated HTML.

Suspected errors were compared with extracted PDF text and selected rendered source pages. Page-marker coverage confirms traceability, not word-for-word completeness. The review did not visually compare every page, audit audio recordings, or run a browser inspection of the Docsify site. A native Malayalam teacher’s review remains valuable for the unresolved grammatical passages and nuanced social usage.
