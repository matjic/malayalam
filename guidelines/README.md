# Textbook editorial guidelines

Write for university students learning Malayalam, including heritage learners with varied literacy backgrounds and students without linguistics training. Preserve academic depth while making prerequisites explicit. These principles guide teaching additions and revisions; scale their application to the learning need rather than repeating a fixed lesson template. Sources and limits are in [research](research.md).

## Give each lesson a useful purpose

State what learners should be able to understand or do, and choose explanations, examples, and practice that serve that purpose. Include meaningful Malayalam use across the course: listening, speaking, reading, and writing. A reference section can support lookup without becoming a complete lesson.

## Build on what learners know

Introduce new demands in manageable steps. Supply the vocabulary, script support, models, and prior concepts needed for the task. Define technical terms in plain language at first instructional use; a glossary link supplements the explanation. Supported texts may include later forms without requiring learners to master them. Connect script, sound, and meaning, and make any romanization a bridge toward Malayalam reading. Provide ways to review or extend material without assuming that heritage speakers already read the script or that every learner knows English grammar.

## Explain meaning through examples

Show what a construction means and when it is useful before detailing how to form it. Use clear, contextual examples and natural translations; add contrasts, segmentation, or diagrams when they resolve a likely difficulty. Distinguish grammatical forms from sentence roles, and explain the limits of comparisons with English or other languages. State the scope of a rule and relevant exceptions. Keep the main explanation focused; put exhaustive detail in reference material.

## Give learners practice and a way to check progress

Balance explanation with supported interpretation and production. Use drills where helpful, alongside tasks that require understanding and meaningful choices. Provide clear instructions and models, answers with reasons where useful, and sample responses or success criteria for open tasks. Make independent practice possible. Return to earlier vocabulary and structures in new contexts, including opportunities to recall and use familiar material more readily. Choose the sequence to suit the task.

## Make the book easy to navigate and use

Use consistent terminology, descriptive headings, readable tables, and useful cross-references. Distinguish core learning from optional depth. Keep essential explanations available in the website, EPUB, and PDF. Use selectable text for ordinary examples, text alternatives for informative visuals, and transcripts for recordings; do not rely on color or hover alone. Check representative rendered pages. Where possible, ask learners to try an explanation and activity; revise where they get stuck.

## Be accurate, respectful, and traceable

Verify new Malayalam examples, translations, and usage judgments against reliable Malayalam evidence or qualified review; record uncertainty. Explain register, regional variation, and social context when they affect use. Represent people and cultures without stereotypes. Identify substantive editorial additions and corrections, preserve source attribution, and contextualize historical claims. Never invent citations, recordings, or speaker review. Follow [repository instructions](../AGENTS.md) for source markers, metadata, and validation.

Before accepting a change, check whether learners can see its purpose, follow it with the support supplied, attempt the task, and understand how they did. Add material only when it improves that experience.
