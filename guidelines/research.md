# Sources and limits

Reviewed 2026-11-02. This is a focused set of professional and institutional guidance, not a systematic review or evidence that one textbook method is best. The [editorial guidelines](README.md) are this project's synthesis.

- **Language learning:** ACTFL's [backward design](https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/guiding-principles-for-language-learning/plan-with-backward-design) connects goals, evidence of learning, and activities. Its [grammar guidance](https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/guiding-principles-for-language-learning/teach-grammar-as-a-concept-in-context) connects forms to meaning and use. We adopt these design principles without prescribing a discovery-only method, rejecting explicit explanation, or converting classroom target-language recommendations into a quota for textbook prose. The grammar page's broad claims about learning effects are not treated here as settled empirical findings.
- **University textbooks:** BCcampus's [Textbook Outline](https://opentextbc.ca/selfpublishguide/chapter/textbook-outline/) addresses audience, organization, learning outcomes, self-testing, and key terms, with features selected for the book's needs. The Open Textbook Library's [review rubric](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/reviews/rubric) supplies broader quality checks: accuracy, clarity, consistency, organization, navigation, and cultural relevance. These are useful review criteria, not a requirement to include every possible textbook feature.
- **Accessibility:** The BCcampus [Accessibility Toolkit checklist](https://opentextbc.ca/accessibilitytoolkit/back-matter/appendix-checklist-for-accessibility-toolkit/) gives concrete checks for headings, images, links, tables, and multimedia. Our brief policy points to these checks; it does not certify that the current edition meets them all.

Apply course-level recommendations across the book, not mechanically to every grammar note. Choose pacing, scaffolding, and review through editorial judgment and learner feedback; these sources do not establish a Malayalam-specific sequence or fixed numerical limits.

Other-language textbooks can suggest teaching approaches. They cannot verify Malayalam grammar, pronunciation, register, or translations. Use Malayalam evidence for those claims, distinguish documented usage from editorial advice, and identify unreviewed judgments. Lesson-specific follow-up belongs in the [proofreading review](../docs/proofreading-review.md), not in standing policy.
